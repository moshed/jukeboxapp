How to Run a Party Playlist Everyone Controls

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

A great party playlist isn't one person's taste on repeat — it's the whole room's. Here's how to run a party playlist everyone actually controls, and why it beats a shared playlist.

The short answer

Skip the single shared playlist and use a group jukebox app. The host starts a session; every guest joins on their phone and can add songs and vote on the queue. The most-voted songs play next, so the room controls the playlist together, live. Jukebox is a free iPhone app built for this.

The problem with a shared playlist

A collaborative Spotify or Apple Music playlist sounds democratic, but it isn't: everyone can add songs, yet nobody controls the order. One friend dumps 20 tracks, another deletes yours, and the vibe never settles. There's no way for the room to say "play this one next."

How a group jukebox fixes it

  • Everyone adds songs — guests request any track from the catalog.
  • Everyone votes — the queue is ranked by votes, so the room's favorites rise to the top.
  • Everyone can skip — a group skip-vote moves past a track nobody's feeling.
  • It's live — vote counts and the queue update in real time for every guest.

The result is a playlist that genuinely reflects the room — not one person's aux cord.

How to set it up (2 minutes)

  1. The host opens Jukebox and starts a session from any Apple Music playlist or their library.
  2. Share the 6-digit code or QR — or let guests join automatically by being nearby.
  3. Guests add songs and vote. The most-voted track plays next. That's it.

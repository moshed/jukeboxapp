A Rockbot Alternative for Parties at Your Place

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

People find Rockbot in a bar, like the idea of picking the music from their phone, and then go looking for it at home. This is the part that catches everyone out: Rockbot is not that kind of app, and no setting will make it that kind of app.

The short answer

Rockbot is a commercial jukebox service for venues. Bars, restaurants and gyms subscribe to it, and the phone app lets customers pick songs at those venues. It is licensed background music for businesses, and it needs the venue's own Rockbot system on the other end.

Jukebox is the home version of that feeling: your own party, your own Apple Music, and your guests voting on what plays next from their phones.

Why Rockbot cannot work at home

It is a licensing question, not a technical one. Playing music publicly in a business requires commercial licences that a personal Apple Music or Spotify account does not carry. Rockbot exists to solve that for venues, and the whole service is built around a paying business at the other end.

A house party is the opposite situation. You already have the right to play your own music at home, so what you actually need is a way to let the room choose it.

Voting instead of paying

This is the biggest difference in how a night feels. Venue jukeboxes typically run on credits — whoever spends the most pushes their song up the queue. It works commercially and it means one determined person can own the room.

Jukebox has no credits and no payment for guests. Everyone gets a set number of votes, the most-voted song plays next, and votes come back when a song plays so people keep spending them. The room decides, not the biggest wallet.

What you actually get at home

Guests join with nothing installed

They scan a QR, type a 6-digit code, or just be nearby and join with no code at all. On iPhone an App Clip opens; on any other phone the voting page opens in the browser.

The party on your TV

The free Apple TV app shows what is playing, the queue with live vote counts, synced lyrics and a join QR — the closest thing to a jukebox standing in the corner of the room. The music keeps playing from the host's phone, so your speakers are never taken over.

A recap at the end

When it finishes, everyone sees what played, how the votes fell, and whose picks the room wanted. Shareable as an image.

If you actually run a venue

Then Rockbot is the correct product and this article is not for you. Commercial premises need commercial licensing, and Jukebox plays a personal Apple Music account, which does not cover public performance in a business.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Rockbot at my own house party?

No. It only works at venues that subscribe to Rockbot. For your own party you need an app that plays from your own music subscription.

What is the home equivalent of Rockbot?

A party voting app such as Jukebox: the host plays from Apple Music and guests vote on the next song from their phones with nothing to install.

Do guests pay per song?

Not with Jukebox. Voting is free for everyone. Venue jukeboxes commonly use paid credits instead.

Can I use Jukebox in my bar?

Not as a substitute for a licensed venue system. It plays a personal Apple Music account, which does not cover public performance in a business.
